Title: Conversation reference: your work at Kestrel
Subtitle: For the Service Manager conversation with the owners
For: Teresa (fictional example)
Level: Independent
Time: Use any time. Talk through one story a day.

# How to use this reference
This is a reference, not a script. It holds the facts and stories you have confirmed, so you can talk about them comfortably in your own words.
! Pick one story, read the cues, then talk about it out loud for five minutes from the angle given. Try a different angle the next day.

# Your work in one breath
Say this idea in your own words, out loud, three different ways:
- Nine years at Kestrel. Lead technician since 2022.
- The hard calls, and the apprentices.
- Ready to lead the service team.

# Story 1: Four apprentices
## Cues
- Four apprentices. Three passed the journeyman exam on the first try.
- "Explain the diagnosis to me before you touch anything."
## Angles to try
- How you train someone who is nervous.
- What you would change about training for 9 technicians and 3 apprentices.
- The apprentice who did not pass the first time: what did you learn?

# Story 2: The pre-departure checklist
## Cues
- 10-point check before leaving every job.
- Callbacks about 8% down to under 4% in a year (your estimate, from the monthly report).
- Other technicians copied it.
## Angles to try
- How you would use it to review the team's tickets.
- How you would introduce it to a technician who thinks it is a waste of time.
! You are not sure if the other technicians' callbacks also dropped. Say that if asked. Do not guess.

# Story 3: Two weeks on dispatch
## Cues
- January 2025 cold snap. Service coordinator on leave.
- No-heat calls first. You called customers yourself when crews ran late.
## Angles to try
- How you decided what came first.
- What the office side taught you about the field side.
- What you would do differently with more time to plan.

# Things you have not done yet
Practice saying these plainly and calmly:
- Reporting department results to the owners.
- Hiring decisions and budgets.
> "I haven't done that yet. What I have done is... and I'd want to learn it by..."

# Questions for Gail and Martin
1. "How would on-call coordination work, and how often would it be my turn?"
2. "What would you want the service department to look like in a year?"
3. "How much of the week would be in the office, and how much in the field?"
- Follow-up if they mention evenings: "How often does that happen?"
Say each question out loud until it sounds like you.

# Unprepared practice
Talk this through out loud with no preparation. There is no single right answer.
"It is the first very cold week of winter. Two technicians are out sick and the board is full of no-heat calls. A long-time commercial customer is angry about a late arrival. What do you do in the next hour?"
Say what you would need to know, what you would do first, and why. When you use something from your two weeks on dispatch, say so.
