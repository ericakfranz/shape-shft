How to use this guide

The practice is talking out loud to yourself. Read a prompt, then start speaking. Reading, thinking or writing alone is not the exercise.

  • You do not need to sound polished. Stumbling, pausing, repeating yourself and going off on tangents are all part of it.
  • Keep talking even if you are not sure where the story is going yet.
  • If you lose your train of thought, stop for a moment, then keep going. There is a list of ways to restart at the end.
  • Keep this guide open and look at it as often as you need. You do not need to remember any of it.
  • If you can, take the practice outside for an easy walk somewhere you feel comfortable talking to yourself. Stop somewhere safe before you read your phone. Practicing at home, sitting or standing, works just as well.
  • Afterward, tell your AI coach what you remembered and where you got stuck.

Dale Brennan, Director of Distribution

What his role suggests

A director of distribution usually cares about the whole operation: shipping on time, cost, accuracy, safety, and whether the manager can lead supervisors. This is an inference from his title, not something he has said.

Stories to explore out loud

  • The receiving checklist (S1): talk about results and how they were measured. Keep it to two minutes.
  • Peak season (S2): talk about planning ahead and keeping promises to stores.
  • Training new hires (S4): talk about getting people productive quickly, which the recruiter said they care about.

Possible concerns and honest ways to talk about them

  • Labor budget: you built schedules but did not own a budget. Practice saying that plainly, then what you did do and how you would learn.

"I haven't owned a labor budget yet. What I did do was build the weekly schedule to match volume, so I understand the trade-offs between hours and deadlines. I'd want to learn how Larkspur builds its budget."

  • Leading supervisors: you supervised associates, not other supervisors. Talk about how you trained and coached leads informally.
  • Why you are looking: the site closed. Keep it short, calm and forward-looking.

Example only. Use your own words.

Cedar Ridge closed our distribution center in August when the company combined two warehouses, so the whole site was let go. I'm looking for a role where I can do more of what I did best there, fixing processes and developing people, with a bigger part of the operation.

Questions to ask

Ask Dale first (most important to you)

  1. "What does a normal on-call week look like, and what kinds of issues come up?"
  2. "How much of this role is the labor budget, and how would I learn your approach?"
  3. "What would a great first six months look like to you?"

More questions if there is time

  • "How did the team handle peak season last year?"
  • "How are Saturday shifts shared during peak?"
  • "How do you and the operations manager split decisions?"

Ask the supervisors

  • "What gets in the way of a good shift here?"
  • "What do you wish the last manager had done differently?"

Natural follow-ups

  • If Dale mentions overtime: "How often does that happen, and how is it planned?"
  • If the budget sounds large: "Is there someone who would teach me the budget process?"

Say each question out loud two or three times so it sounds like you.

If you get stuck

Pick any of these and say it out loud. Then keep going. They are tools for thinking, not scripts.

  • "The reason that mattered was..."
  • "What led me to that decision was..."
  • "What made that especially hard was..."
  • "What that meant for the people involved was..."
  • "My part in it was..."
  • "Looking back, I think..."
  • "Another way I could have handled it was..."
  • "That reminds me of another time when..."
  • "The main point I am trying to make is..."

Ways to restart

  • Pause. Take a breath. You do not need to apologize.
  • Start the story again from a different point, for example from the end.
  • Ask yourself a question out loud: "Why did that matter?"
  • Describe one small detail: a place, a person, a sound, a tool.
  • Say the same idea in different words.
