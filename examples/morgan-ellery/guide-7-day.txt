Title: 7-day practice: Talking about your work for the Larkspur job
Subtitle: Operations Manager, Larkspur Outdoor Supply
For: Morgan (fictional example)
Level: Guided on days 1 to 3, Supported on days 4 to 7
Time: About 15 minutes a day

# How this week works
Each day has one short spoken exercise. The first days give you more help. The later days give you less, so you start talking on your own.
- Days 1 to 3 revisit stories you know.
- Days 4 and 5 cover the topics you find harder: leadership and a mistake.
- Day 6 connects everything to Larkspur.
- Day 7 returns to Day 2 from a new angle.
! Each day: read the prompt, then talk out loud. Keep the guide open. You can look at it as often as you want.

# Day 1: Explain your job to a stranger
Imagine a neighbor asks, "So what did you actually do at the warehouse?" Talk for five minutes.
> "Cedar Ridge was a distribution center, which means..."
> "On second shift, my job was to..."
> "A good night looked like... A bad night looked like..."
Then explain it again as if the neighbor is 12 years old. Simple words force you to understand what matters.
::: open Facts you might use
- Regional center for a home goods retailer, about 40 stores.
- Second shift, 2 pm to 10:30 pm: receiving, picking, loading.
- Crew of 12 to 18 associates.
:::

# Day 2: The checklist, from memory
Talk about the receiving checklist for three minutes **before** you open the facts below. Then open them and notice what you left out. Talk for two more minutes, adding the missing pieces.
::: closed Facts you told your coach (open after you speak)
- Records did not match the shelves; store orders went out short.
- Two weeks watching all three shifts.
- One-page checklist. Six receivers trained.
- About 40% fewer discrepancies in six months (roughly 50 a week to roughly 30, your estimate).
- Became standard on all shifts.
:::
> "What made that especially hard was..."

# Day 3: Where the number came from
Interviewers often ask, "How did you measure that?" Last time, this is where you got stuck. Talk it through out loud:
- Who made the report? (The inventory control manager.)
- How often? (Weekly.)
- What did it count?
- What was it before and after?
- What else changed during those six months that might have helped?
> "The way we tracked it was..."
> "I want to be careful about the number, because..."
! Saying "I am not sure exactly how the report counted that" is honest and fine. Do not fill the gap with a guess.

# Day 4: Training people (Supported)
Talk about training new hires. You trained about 30 people over four years (your estimate).
- How did you train someone on their first shift?
- What did people find hardest?
- Tell one story about a specific new hire.
- What would you teach a new supervisor about training?
Watch for "we" when you mean "I". Try the bridge: "My part in it was..."

# Day 5: A change that did not work (Supported)
In 2022 you moved fast-selling items closer to the dock without telling first shift, and their pickers went to the old locations.
- What happened the next morning?
- What did you do to fix it?
- What do you do differently now?
Talk about it calmly and briefly. Owning a mistake shows judgment. You do not need to apologize for it now.

# Day 6: Larkspur, and something new (Supported)
Larkspur's center opened in 2023 and is growing. The posting says the manager will help prepare the labor budget, which you have not done before.
Part 1: talk about how you would spend your first month as Operations Manager.
Part 2, practice without preparation: "It is October. Volume is rising fast and two associates quit this week. Talk through what you would do." There is no single right answer. Say what you would need to know, the options, and what you would try first.
! Keep "what I would do" separate from "what I have done". When you use your Cedar Ridge experience, say so: "At Cedar Ridge, what worked was..."

# Day 7: The checklist, for a director (Independent)
Tell the checklist story again, but this time to Dale Brennan, Director of Distribution, in two minutes. A director cares about results, cost and risk.
- Cues: short orders, three shifts, one page, six people, about 40%, standard everywhere.
Then reflect:
- What is easier now than on Day 1?
- Which story do you want to practice again?
- What new facts did you remember this week?
Bring your answers back to your coach so it can update your files and plan next week.
