# How Conversation Coach practice works

The Conversation Coach helps you get comfortable talking about your work, in your own words. It is not a list of perfect interview answers to memorize.

## The practice is talking out loud

The main exercise is simple: **you talk out loud to yourself about your work.**

Reading prompts, thinking quietly, or writing answers can help, but they are not the practice. Speaking is. Saying things out loud is how you find out what you actually remember, where you get stuck, and how your own words sound.

- Read a prompt, then start talking.
- Keep talking, even if you are not sure where the story is going.
- Stumbling, pausing, repeating yourself and going off on tangents are normal. They are part of learning.
- First find out what you want to say. Make it shorter and clearer later.

You do not need an AI voice feature. Talking to yourself is a complete method. Afterward, come back to your AI coach and type what you noticed or remembered. The coach does not hear your practice unless you share a recording or a transcript.

If speaking out loud is difficult for you, for any reason, tell your coach. There are other ways to practice the same way, such as whispering, talking with someone you trust, or typing quickly without editing.

## Walk and talk

If it suits you, practice during an easy walk somewhere familiar and quiet. Many people find it easier to talk when they are moving. Keep your guide open on your phone, and stop somewhere safe before you read it. Practicing at home works just as well.

## Practice with real jobs

Practice is based on real opportunities: jobs you applied for, are considering, or tried for before. You talk about the same experiences in the context of different jobs, so you learn to use them flexibly, not as one memorized answer.

## Three levels of support

| Guided | Supported | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| Full explanations, the facts, many sentence starters, small steps, and examples | Shorter facts, some prompts, more freedom | Short reminders and broad topics |

You can change level at any time, and use different levels for different topics. Needing more help with one topic is normal.

## Your coach will sometimes disagree with you

A good coach is supportive and honest. Your coach may ask what you did yourself when you say "we", ask how a number was measured, or point out when you are underselling your work. It does this to help you, not to criticize you.

## What the coach will not do

- Invent experience you do not have.
- Give you scores, ratings or personality judgments.
- Decide for you whether to take a job.
- Promise an interview or an offer.
